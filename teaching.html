Teaching Assistantships

Econ 2010 — Principles of Microeconomics

An introductory course in microeconomics with over 600 students. In Spring 2026 I served as Head TA, managing a team of 10 undergraduate and graduate TAs.

Ken Elzinga and Carter Doyle, University of Virginia. Fall 2022, Spring 2024, Spring 2026.

Econ 2559 — Economics of Gender at Work

A newly developed course aimed at students outside the economics major. I worked with Professor Miller to shape the course for that audience and built supplemental material to make it more engaging and accessible. Nominated for the University-Wide Graduate Teaching Award.

Amalia Miller, University of Virginia. Fall 2023, Fall 2024.

Econ 3010 — Intermediate Microeconomics

A demanding, math-heavy core requirement that often catches students whose quantitative skills are rusty. It's the most office-hours-intensive course I teach, working through material one-on-one in a style quite different from my other classes.

Maria Westerfield and Marc Santugini, University of Virginia. Spring 2023, Spring 2025.

Econ 3720 — Economics of the Public Sector

An advanced course for some of the department's strongest undergraduates, centered on learning to think like an economist. I taught it the first semester the department adapted the course for AI in the classroom, working with Professor Lockwood to keep it effective.

Lee Lockwood, University of Virginia. Fall 2025.

Other Teaching

Leader, Microeconomics Core Exam Prep

A three-week crash course preparing first-year PhD students for their core exams, which they must pass to remain in the program. The material is dense enough that it regularly stumps me as much as the students — it's an intense, highly collaborative few weeks working through it together.

University of Virginia. 2024–2026.

Undergraduate TA, Department of Mathematics

Sat in on Algebra, Trigonometry, and Calculus I–II courses, helping students work through problems in class. Explaining the same concept several different ways for a room with a huge range of ability was the main skill this job demanded.

University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. 2018–2021.

ACT Prep Math Tutor

Mostly one-on-one work helping students build problem-solving strategies, but occasionally taught full classes of high schoolers, which meant building lesson plans designed to cover as much ground as possible in a one-week window.

A Plus Academic Coaching. 2017–2018.
